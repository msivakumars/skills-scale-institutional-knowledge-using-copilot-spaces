# OctoAcme — Project Management Processes

OctoAcme runs projects through a clear, stage-gated lifecycle: Initiation → Planning → Execution → Release → Close/Retrospective. Initiation focuses on validating the business need with a Project One-pager that captures problem, goals, success metrics, stakeholders, and a decision gate. Planning turns approved initiatives into a prioritized, estimated backlog, establishes a Definition of Done, and captures initial risks and dependencies in a Risk Register so the team has a shared plan before execution begins.

Day-to-day work follows a disciplined workflow on a project board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done) and a strict PR process. Pull requests are expected to be small when practical, reference the related issue and acceptance criteria, run CI (tests and lint) before review, and require signoff per team policy. Sprint planning is timeboxed; items pulled into a sprint must meet the Definition of Done and have clear acceptance criteria. Releases follow a checklist (CI/security scans, smoke testing in staging, rollback plan, release notes) to reduce risk and ensure observability.

Roles and responsibilities are explicit: Product Managers define outcomes and success metrics, Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication, Developers implement and test, QA validates acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide inputs and approvals. Communication cadence includes daily standups focused on progress and blockers, weekly delivery syncs, PM+PdM alignment meetings, and monthly stakeholder updates. Escalation paths (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) and incident communication templates support predictable responses for blockers and incidents.

Quality assurance and continuous improvement are embedded in the workflow. Developers are expected to provide unit and integration tests, run end-to-end smoke tests on critical flows, and use security scanning in CI. Manual QA is used where appropriate for acceptance. Retrospectives are timeboxed to produce a short list of prioritized action items that are tracked back into the backlog; the Risk Register and weekly syncs keep risk mitigation visible and current.
